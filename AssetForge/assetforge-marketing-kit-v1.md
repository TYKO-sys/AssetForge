## 1. Platform verdicts (honest)

### Substack — park it
- Agents do not hang out there. They are on Instagram and in brokerage Facebook groups.
- A newsletter takes 6+ months of consistency before it moves revenue. Your plan needs first clients in weeks, not seasons.
- The newsletter asset in your pack is something you make FOR clients, not how you win them.
- Revisit when: you expand to the podcasters niche (they read newsletters), or month 3+ when referrals need a nurture channel.

### Fiverr — yes, as a side door
- Buyers arrive with intent, searching exactly what the machine outputs. Zero cost to list.
- Treat it as paid practice: real orders, real feedback, prompt tuning data.
- Reviews become reusable proof for direct outreach ("check my reviews" beats "trust me").
- The catch: 20% fee and a price-shopper culture. Never let gig prices anchor your retainer — keep one-off gig pricing consistent with retainer math so buyers can graduate upward.

### Gumroad — park it
- It sells products. You sell a service. Wrong door until productization (Year 2 plan, Layer 4).
- Signal to watch for: clients asking "can I just buy your templates/captions?" That question is the door opening. Until someone asks, building a product is a distraction from the retainer.

### Where the buyers actually are (from the plan, Table 7)
- Instagram local hashtags + DMs (the demo-first lane)
- Brokerage Facebook groups
- YouTube "listing tour [city]" searches and public agent directories (lead lists, free)
- LinkedIn for brokers and team leads
- Referral loop: one free month per introduction

## 6. The apprentice path (beginner → great)

### Stage 1 — Learn to see the month inside one video
The skill: watch a video once and know what is extractable. Most people see a video. You will see 30 days of material.

Task: 15 minutes a day, one agent video from your city, pen and paper. Write five things you would pull: a hook, a quote, a clip moment, a number, a question the video answers.

Drill: take your favorite local agent's best video and write the five pieces as finished captions by hand. No AI. This builds the taste the machine cannot build for you.

Uncommon resources: your lead list is your swipe file — the 20 best agent accounts in your market, studied weekly, teach you the local voice. Comment sections under big real estate coaching channels (Tom Ferry, Brandon Mulrenin, Krista Mashore) are free market research; agents post their exact frustrations there daily.

The grader: once the acceptance run passes, feed the same video through the machine and compare its picks to yours. The machine grades your eye. That loop is the fastest marketing education available to you, and it is already built.

### Stage 2 — Hooks: the first line does 80% of the work
The skill: writing first lines that stop a thumb mid-scroll.

Task: never keep the first line you write. Write ten. Say each one out loud. Keep the one you would actually say to a friend at a listing appointment.

Drill: collect 20 reels that stopped your thumb this week. Write down the exact first line of each. The pattern will jump out, and then it becomes yours.

Uncommon resource: stand-up comedy openers. A comic's first five seconds exist for one job — buying the next thirty. Identical job to your caption's first line. Watch how the good ones open.

Practice: rewrite the three weakest captions you can find from local agents. Privately. You are training your eye, not posting criticism.

### Stage 3 — The demo is the pitch
The skill: outreach that never feels like outreach, because the gift arrives before the ask.

Task: run the weekly rhythm — five packs, two-line notes, one simple tracking sheet (name, video, sent date, reply).

Simulations — write your answers before reality asks them:
- "How much?" → say the number fast and plain. $297 for two a month. A straight price makes people trust everything else you say.
- "Who else have you done this for?" → honesty wins: "You'd be a founding client. First pack is free so you can judge the work, not my pitch."
- "Can you do my market updates weekly?" → "Yes — that's the four-video plan, $497. Want the first one free?"
- Silence → one bump in three weeks, bring something new, then let it go. No guilt trips. The sheet remembers so you do not have to.

Uncommon resource: public agent directories plus "[your city] listing tour" searches give you an endless, pre-sorted lead list for free. Five a week is a drop in that ocean.

### Stage 4 — Delivery becomes marketing
The skill: turning every shipped pack into proof that sells the next one.

Task: every pack you deliver becomes three of your own posts — the before and after, the behind the scenes, the result. Your feed turns into a permanent demo, which is exactly the proof-of-work angle in the plan.

The referral ask: one free month per introduction, asked in the same message as a delivered pack, never separately. A happy client refers when the gift is fresh.

Simulation: draft the referral ask that does not feel needy. The test: would you forward it if you were the client? If yes, it ships.

### Shortcuts
1. The machine writes your feed too. Proof of work while you sleep.
2. Postiz batches a month of your own posts in one sitting.
3. Comment first, DM second. A real comment (not an emoji), wait a day, then the note. Reply rates jump because you arrive as a person, not a pitch.
4. Fiverr reviews double as outreach proof. Link them, never pay for fake ones.

## 7. DM reply scripts (launch day, pre-written)

Both captions end with "DM me PACK." That means interested agents land in your inbox within hours of posting. Three rules: reply within a few hours (speed reads as competence on a solo service), keep every DM short, never argue with a no. Edit these so they sound like you — keep the shape.

**1. They comment PACK (public reply first — the algorithm watches comments):**
"Just DM'd you — check your requests."
Then the opener:
"Hi [Name] — thanks for the comment. Send a link to any video where you're talking, five minutes or longer. I'll build your free pack from it, no strings. What area do you farm?"

**2. "How much?"**
"Free for your first pack — not a teaser, the whole thing, built from your own video. If you want to keep going after: $297/month for two videos fully processed, $497 for four plus priority turnaround. Month to month, pause anytime. Want the free one first?"

**3. "Is this AI content?"**
"The heavy lifting is automated — I built the system myself. But every line passes my eyes before you see it, and every quote gets checked against your actual video. If it can't be traced to something you really said, it doesn't ship. Send any video and judge the output, not the label."

**4. "My nephew edits my videos."**
"Keep the nephew — family rates are unbeatable. This covers the other weeks, when he's busy or you've got more footage than hours. Send one video he hasn't touched and compare. Free."

**5. "Just send me the info / a link."**
"There's no link, on purpose. I'd rather show you than pitch you: one video in, a full pack back, free, yours to keep even if we never talk again. If you like it, the monthly details are simple — $297 or $497."

**6. The pack handoff:**
"Your pack's ready: captions, quote lines, clip timestamps, a blog draft, show notes, audio, and titles. Two asks — post your favorite this week, and tell me honestly what feels off. That feedback is the whole reason the first pack is free."

**7. The ghost bump (day 3, no reply):**
"No pressure — DMs bury themselves. The offer stands: one video in, a full pack out, free. Want one of this week's five spots?"

**8. The final touch (day 7):**
"Last nudge, promise. I'm filling this week's five free spots — if the timing's wrong, no worries at all. If you want in, send any video and I'll start tonight."

The five-spots line stays in every script because it's true — it's your actual weekly capacity from the plan. Honest scarcity is the only kind that survives repeat customers.

## 8. The first customer sprint (the call: run the engine this week)

Every asset in this kit points at one thing that doesn't exist yet: a finished pack, built by the machine, on your laptop. The captions can't go live honestly until it exists. So the next move isn't more copy — it's the Stage 1 run. It costs nothing but electricity, runs while you sleep, and the run log doubles as degree evidence. The "which platform feels alive" question answers itself the day real packs exist.

**Night 1 — install (about 45 min, half of it downloads):**
1. Unzip assetforge-v1.0.1.zip on the Windows laptop.
2. Open PowerShell in the folder and run: `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\setup-windows.ps1`
3. When it finishes, restart the Ollama tray app (the script sets variables that only new processes see).
4. Run the smoke test: `.venv\Scripts\python.exe af.py smoke` — the laptop's own voice becomes a full test pack. If it talks and hands you a folder of assets, the machine works.

**Night 2 — the real run:**
5. Pick your first video: anything with you talking, five minutes or more. The one sitting in your camera roll works fine. On 8 GB RAM keep the first one under 30 minutes; 16 GB can take a full episode.
6. Submit it before bed: `.venv\Scripts\python.exe af.py submit C:\path\to\video.mp4 --client test-agent` — then let it run (run-daemon.bat). On CPU, 2–5 hours is normal and unattended. That's the design working, not a problem.
7. Morning: check the outbox folder and open manifest.json. Pass bar on Windows: it finished on its own, the provenance verdicts are clean (nothing invented), and the contents read like your video.

**Pass-day rewards — pre-decided so you decide nothing:**
- Post Caption 1 (The Math).
- Flip the Fiverr gig from draft to live.
- Start the Monday rhythm: five agents, two-line notes (Section 4).
- Tell me it passed, so we move to the plan's next phase with proof in hand.

**If anything goes red:** copy the exact error text and paste it into our chat. Three known gotchas, all pre-solved: restart the Ollama tray app after setup, install the Microsoft Visual C++ Redistributable (x64) if faster-whisper complains during install, and if winget is missing on very old Windows 10, install Ollama, FFmpeg, yt-dlp, and uv by hand, then re-run the script.
